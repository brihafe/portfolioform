Portfolio Submission Guide

Here's a quick guide to help you confidently complete your premium portfolio submission.

Beginners Are Welcome!

You do not need years of experience or a massive client list to build a stunning portfolio. Mock projects, practice work, school assignments, personal brands, and passion projects all count. What matters is how you present your skills and communicate your value.

Full Name: Use your real full name. This builds trust with potential clients.

Professional Name: Can be your branded online identity if you use a different name for business.

Positioning Statement: One clear sentence explaining what you help clients achieve.

Example

"I help coaches and online businesses streamline operations through efficient admin and social media support."

Bio (3-5 sentences): Share your background, relevant experiences, strengths, and personality.

Beginner-Friendly Note

If you're new to freelancing or virtual assistance, you can still build a strong portfolio. Focus on your skills, strengths, training, mock projects, and willingness to learn.

List services you genuinely know or are actively learning. Examples include:

Social Media Management
Lead Generation
Executive Assistance
Graphic Design
Video Editing
Email Management
Appointment Setting
Data Entry
Customer Support

Beginner-Friendly Note

You do not need years of experience. If you've practiced these skills through training, mock projects, personal pages, internships, school organizations, or helping friends/family businesses, those experiences still count.

Select tools you are familiar with, even at beginner level. Clients appreciate honesty and willingness to learn.

Beginner-Friendly Note

If you created sample designs in Canva, organized tasks in Notion, edited videos in CapCut, or used Google Workspace for school or practice projects, those are valid experiences.

Upload or link any of the following via Google Drive:

Google Drive folders
Canva designs
Sample social posts
Mock brand projects
Practice websites
Presentation decks
Video edits
Admin systems

What is a Case Study?

A case study is a sample project where you explain the goal, your strategy/process, and the result or expected outcome. Even practice projects can become strong portfolio pieces when presented professionally.

Beginner-Friendly Note

If you do not have real clients yet, mock projects and case studies are completely acceptable. Examples: sample Instagram content calendar, mock lead generation tracker, practice branding project, demo executive assistant workflow, fictional business social media strategy.

Testimonials can come from:

Previous clients
Internship supervisors
Teachers
Classmates
Business owners
Volunteer work

Beginner-Friendly Note

If you don't have testimonials yet, you may leave this blank. You can always add them later as you gain experience.

Use professional and active links only. Recommendations:

  • Use a professional email (avoid old or casual addresses)
  • Make sure all links are accessible and not broken
  • Ensure your Facebook or LinkedIn profile looks presentable
  • Set your social profiles to public or accessible so clients can view them

Be honest about your availability, timezone, rates, and response time.

Beginner-Friendly Note

Beginners may start with entry-level pricing while building confidence and experience. It's better to start with competitive rates and raise them as you grow than to overprice and underdeliver.

Use a photo that meets these criteria:

Clear, high-quality image
Neutral or clean background
Professional appearance
Friendly smile preferred

A professional-looking photo helps establish trust and personal branding. You don't need a studio shot — a well-lit phone photo against a plain wall works perfectly.

Remember This

You do not need to be perfect to start building your portfolio.

Many successful Virtual Assistants started with mock projects, practice work, small freelance tasks, personal brand experiments, and volunteer experiences.
